CF001 — WHAT IS COMPUTE FUND (Leah, script 1 of the education series)
Topic: why the platform exists. ~1,250 words ≈ 7 min.

(bright, friendly) Hi, I'm Leah. I'm a digital voice, and I work for Compute Fund. Today, I want to tell you what this platform actually is. Not the marketing version. The real one. Because what we're building here is simple to say and radical to mean: a place where open source gets funded, and every dollar leaves a receipt.

(warm, steady) Open source built the modern world. Your phone runs on it. Your bank runs on it. The internet itself runs on it. And the strange, generous truth is that most of the people who built it were never paid. They gave their nights and weekends to the commons, and the commons paid them back in reputation, not rent. Compute Fund exists to change that one project at a time.

(calm, confident) Here is the core of the platform, in one sentence. Money comes in from patrons, it becomes governed compute for creators, and every single run comes back out as a public receipt. Money in, governed compute, receipts out. Those are the three rails everything sits on. If a feature doesn't touch one of those rails, we don't build it yet.

(friendly, steady) Let me make that concrete. You open a project on the platform. You write down one clear, provable question, the thing your project is actually trying to demonstrate. Patrons find your project and fund it with real money. That money doesn't disappear into a wallet with a promise attached. It becomes budget: compute that our gateway dispenses to you, capped at exactly what was donated, against the models the platform allows.

(resolved) And when you run that compute, the platform writes a receipt. What model ran. What it cost. What came out. Every receipt is public, attached to the project, forever. A stranger who arrives at the page tomorrow can look at the full history and decide for themselves whether this project deserves their trust and their dollars. That's the whole trick: we don't ask you to believe us. We show you the numbers.

(contemplative) Why does this matter? Because funding, historically, has been the missing piece of open source. Sharing was solved ages ago: licenses made code free to reuse. Forking was solved: anyone can take a project public and build on it. Monetizing was never solved. Donation buttons exist, but they're a coin toss — a leaky bucket at the door of people doing serious work. Compute Fund is the funding layer: patronage that directly becomes the compute a project needs to move.

(low, controlled) Let me give you the scale of what's already here. The catalog holds more than eighteen hundred projects. Productivity tools. Big tech open source. Robotics. 3D and animation. Gaming, including the decompilation and preservation work — the rebuilds that keep old consoles alive. Commons — public-domain revival projects. JEV, our routing-agents shelf. Audio. Education. Home. Finance. Every one of them was sourced, verified, and imported with its license checked.

(warm) And here's a number I love: seventeen of those projects are already claimed by their maintainers on the platform. The rest stand ready — unclaimed work waiting for its builder to arrive, prove ownership, and take the wheel. That's deliberate. Unclaimed projects aren't leftovers. They're open doors. If you've ever wanted to adopt a project that already exists, the shelf is full of them.

(resolved) Now, one thing we are NOT. Compute Fund is not an investment vehicle. You don't buy equity. You don't wait for a payout. Patronage on this platform is patronage in the old sense of the word: you back work you want to see exist, and the platform turns that support into real, governed compute. The rules are written down — non-transferable funds, no cash-out, no promises of returns. It's patronage you can audit.

(friendly) Who is this for? For creators: a place where your work gets funded by the community, with the receipts building your reputation as you go. For patrons: a place where your money becomes visible, measurable progress instead of a leap of faith. And for everybody else: a public ledger of what the community is building this week — honestly, run by run.

(contemplative) A quick tour of the shelves, so you know what "open source" means here in practice. The productivity lane alone holds more than two hundred imported tools — writing, planning, data work. Big tech open source — Meta, Google, Microsoft, Alibaba — is its own lane, because the giants quietly give away infrastructure the whole internet runs on. Robotics, 3D and animation, home automation, audio engineering, education, finance. There's a gaming lane with a preservation heart: decompilations that keep the consoles you grew up with running, ported to hardware you own now. And a commons lane reviving films and books whose copyright has expired — work that literally belongs to everyone. Several of these lanes you could have guessed; a few will surprise you. Every row carries its license, checked at import. If a project's rights are unclear, it doesn't get a page.

(warm) That's Compute Fund. The funding layer for open source, built on three rails: money in, governed compute, receipts out. In the next videos I'll walk the mechanics with you — the fork, the rules, the full journey of a project from question to funded. I'm Leah, this is Compute Fund, and I'm glad you're here.
