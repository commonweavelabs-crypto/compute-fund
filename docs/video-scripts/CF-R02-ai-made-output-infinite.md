CF-R02 - "AI MADE OUTPUT INFINITE. HERE'S WHAT'S STILL SCARSE." (Leah, script draft, from Alex M H Smith's methods)
Value theory applied: value = desirability + scarcity; the four corners incl. pollution; fake value = "new not unique"; strategy hierarchy. ~1,250 words = 8 min. Sources: Alex M H Smith's talk + workbook (methods used with attribution).

(bright, friendly) Hi, I'm Leah. Quick question that sounds like a philosophy class but isn't: what is value? A strategist named Alex M H Smith gives the cleanest definition I've found: desirability plus scarcity. Both, together. desirable-but-plentiful is a commodity. scarce-but-unwanted is who cares. And in the age of slop there's a fourth corner - undesirable AND plentiful - and he calls it pollution. Today I want to run three ideas past you. One: AI just made output infinite. Two: that means most of the software world is drifting toward the commodity corner - and honestly, a lot of it is drifting toward pollution. Three: the scarce things left are exactly the things Compute Fund funds. Let me prove it.

(contemplative) First, the drift. For decades, writing good software was scarce. Now, anyone with an agent can ship an app in a weekend, and a whole channel of reels teaches you to replace your subscriptions with vibe-coded clones. Output went from scarce to plentiful overnight. And when the scarce half of the value formula collapses, the formula collapses with it. You can make the most desirable software in the world - if it's one of ten thousand identical clones, it's a commodity. If it's one of the ten thousand that break silently, it's not even a commodity. It's pollution.

(low, grounded) Now the honest test - and his workbook has a trap list for exactly this, because people lie to themselves about the word 'unique'. So use 'new' instead: can you name what you offer that is actually new? We tried it on ourselves. Our trap list score: differentiated process - nobody cares how the sausage is made - guilty, a little: 'verifiable milestones' is only valuable because of the artifact at the end, and we have to keep proving that. Forceful generic value: 'we fund open source' - the whole category says that now. The dreaded USP: we don't have a bolted-on quirk. And the one that stings: nice messaging - you cannot produce value through words alone; you must do it. So let's do it, publicly, and tell you what's actually new.

(calm, confident) The new value is verified care. When output is infinite, three things stay scarce. Provenance: the provable line from an idea to an artifact to the person who made it. Trust: receipts that anyone can re-run. And staying power: someone who actually answers for the thing you depend on. None of these can be generated by an agent - they're expensive on purpose. That's the strategy hierarchy lesson applied to us: our value is 'verified care'; our product is the loop that produces it - question in, milestone, artifact, receipt; our brand is one sentence: trust is a product. Everything that isn't aimed at producing that value, we'd stop. It should be easy to fill out - it is, because the loop is one circle.

(warm, plain) The workbook's three games, played on us, because we believe in doing the homework we recommend. Game one - value mixing: Method took design language from olive oil into cleaning products. What if an open-source funding layer took the language from... an aerospace safety culture? Flight reviews, checklists signed by name, failure analysis that publishes. That's not a word game - that's literally the receipts design. Game two - selling beyond the market: oat milk ignored the oat-milk market and sold lower emissions to coffee drinkers. Who isn't shopping 'open source funding'? The 200 million people who use open tools but never give. The influencer who would steward a project if the stewarding were real. The auditor who'd love a work order instead of a fight. Game three - cultivate a weakness, the backwards one: what are we bad at? Speed and polish. We'll never win the race. So what does being slow unlock? Verification. A funding layer that is deliberately slower, because the checking takes time, is a form of value the fast money literally cannot copy - their model is incompatible with it. That's our moat, and it hurts our competitors to chase it, because verification requires patience, and patience is the thing their model can't buy.

(contemplative) So here's the honest audit sheet, in one breath. Desirability: for the people who build and use open software, verified care is deeply wanted - score it high, and demand proof as we ship. Scarcity: real, because attention, provenance and trust cannot be generated by the agents flooding the market - everyone else is optimizing the exact opposite. Corner: we're in the desired-and-scarce corner, with exactly two competitors worth marking on the chart - GitHub's sponsors, which is a tip jar without accountability, and Patreon-style patronage, which is accountability without the artifact. And the last row of the audit - what we'd stop: anything that doesn't accelerate the loop.

(resolved) That's the whole strategy in plain words. AI made output infinite. Scarcity didn't die - it moved. It moved to provenance, trust, and care. And the funding layer for the scarce half is what we're building.

(low, friendly) I'm Leah, this is Compute Fund. Desirable is not enough. Scarce is not enough. You need both - and now you know which one to build next: the verifiable kind.
