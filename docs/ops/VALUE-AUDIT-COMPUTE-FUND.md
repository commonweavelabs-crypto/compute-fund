# VALUE AUDIT - COMPUTE FUND (per Alex M H Smith's Value Generation workbook)
The 7-step workbook applied to ourselves, honestly (iron law: honest ratings, no smoothing). Source: the free workbook (Alex M H Smith). Done 2026-11-25 to challenge our own assumptions. Re-run quarterly.

## STEP 1 - WHAT YOU OFFER
- 1.1 What we sell: a funding-and-verification layer for open-source maintenance: patrons fund deterministic questions/milestones; governed compute executes; public receipts return.
- 1.2 What people get: (material) maintained, working open-source projects + compute actually delivered; (emotional) trust - "someone answers for this thing"; (identity) being the kind of person who funds open work with proof it landed.
- 1.3 Where else could they get it, and how easy: GitHub Sponsors (easy; tip jar, no accountability); Patreon/BuyMeACoffee (easy; patronage, no artifacts); grants/foundations (hard; slow, opaque); do nothing (easy). HONEST ANSWER: "mostly nowhere else" for the receipts/verification half; "easily" for the donation half. The donation half alone is NOT our value - the verified half is.

## STEP 2 - DESIRABILITY + SCARCITY (scored harshly)
- Desirability: 4/5 today (pre-launch: wanted in principle by loud community consensus, unproven with paying behavior - that missing point is why not 5).
- Scarcity: 4/5 (verification-first patronage genuinely rare; competitors optimize the opposite).
- Corner: Value corner (desired + scarce), with the honest caveat that our position comes from unshipped rails until the money loop runs live.
- Competitors on the chart: GH (high desirability, low scarcity of the donation form), PT (patronage peers, same).

## STEP 3 - THE SEVEN FORMS
- Fix: 3/5 (maintainers solve the "my tool is dying" problem - only once claimed/active).
- Protect: 4/5 (our strongest: security audits + verified milestones = before-the-failure insurance).
- Save: 2/5 (users skip searching for trustworthy maintainers; not yet felt).
- Identity: 3/5 ("public receipts for what I funded" = patron identity with proof).
- Pleasure: honest 1/5 (not our lane).
- Belonging: 2/5 today, buildable (gardener communities + comments-on-releases milestone).
- Meaning/growth: 3/5 (builders become capable via receipted work; patrons become part of the commons).
- Form doing most work today: Protect. Form nobody in the category offers: Protect+Belonging COMBINED (funded verification as a community act).

## STEP 4 - THE SHUFFLE (last 5 years, next 2)
- Used to be scarce, now plentiful: code (agents), design, content, apps themselves.
- Used to be desirable, now less so: raw output ("I built an app"), follower counts, launch-day hype.
- Used to be plentiful, now scarce: provenance, verified care, accountable maintainers, security diligence.
- Used to be unwanted, now wanted: receipts (the Riemann/Ginkgo moment - verification became a story people share).
- Where our value drifts: WITH the shuffle into the bottom-right boxes = our tailwind. Risk: "funding for open source" is simultaneously drifting from scarce (nobody did it) toward crowded (platform clones) - the receipts+governance half is what keeps US scarce.

## STEP 5 - FAKE VALUE TRAPS (the five)
- Differentiated process: PARTIAL GUILT - "deterministic questions" is process; the value is the artifact. Counter: the CFQ exists to force the artifact into the open.
- Forceful generic: "we fund open source" - everyone with a sponsor button says this. Counter: stop leading with it.
- The dreaded USP: none bolted on (clean).
- Shared values: low risk, but "receipts philosophy" becomes values-speak if the ledger never runs - the loop is the proof.
- Nice messaging: the standing temptation - we will not produce value through words alone; this audit itself is the refusal.

## STEP 6 - THE THREE GAMES
- Game 1 VALUE MIXING: generic offerings here = support maintainers, fund projects. Foreign categories borrowed: aerospace safety culture (named sign-offs, published failure analysis -> receipts + public falsified-by); clinical trials (pre-registered questions with falsification criteria -> the CFQ); escrow (money releases only on the verified artifact). Idea worth pulling on: PUBLISHED FAILURE ANALYSIS for falsified milestones - nobody in open-source funding does it.
- Game 2 SELLING BEYOND THE MARKET: category value = support open source. Abstracted: being part of the fix for software you rely on. Who doesn't shop our category: (a) companies consuming open tools who fear audits - sell verified dependency maintenance; (b) educators/influencers - stewarding as funded work (the gardener thesis); (c) auditors - work orders instead of fights; (d) agencies shipping AI slop - receipts as their differentiation. Ostentatious signal: public CFR badges + the audit-receipt marketplace.
- Game 3 CULTIVATE A WEAKNESS: strengths = rigor, receipts, governance. Weaknesses vs competition = speed, polish, marketing muscle. If we were even WORSE at speed: deliberate slowness becomes the product - verification takes the time it takes; fast competitors are structurally incompatible with it (you cannot speed-run an audit). Who values that: everyone burned by the slop wave - the Nisha audience. Named form: "the slow, checkable money."

## STEP 7 - STRATEGY HIERARCHY (one page)
- VALUE: verified care - open software with someone accountable, publicly.
- PRODUCT: the loop (CFQ -> milestone -> governed compute -> artifact -> public receipt) + CFR + takedown/audit mechanics.
- BRAND: "Trust is a product." (message: from toy to tool; bring your receipts)
- What we'd stop: anything not accelerating the loop - breadth features that don't serve it, pre-launch polish that doesn't serve it, any metric that measures engagement without an artifact.

## COMPETITIVE POSITION (self-reflection)
- GitHub: ally + rails; Sponsors = the donation half without artifacts. Not a fight - a dependency.
- Patreon-style peers: emotion without verification; complements rather than competes if they ever accept artifact proof.
- The real competitor: DOING NOTHING (the default world where maintenance is unpaid). Our enemy is inertia, not a platform.
