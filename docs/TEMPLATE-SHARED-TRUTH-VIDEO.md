## THE TEN BEATS

### T1 · THE MIRROR QUESTION (0:00-0:45, ~5% of runtime)
- Mechanics: open on the viewer's own polarity — "do you hate AI or love
  it?" — then VALIDATE BOTH SIDES and place the audience in the middle
  ("most people are somewhere in the middle: uncertain, uneasy"). One line
  of internet-culture acknowledgment ("have you been online lately?") for
  the smile.
- Why it works: it defuses the culture war BEFORE it starts; nobody is the
  enemy; the viewer sees themselves, not a strawman. The hook = a mirror,
  not bait.
- CF application: "Do you think AI is eating your craft — or finally
  paying for it?" → both sides honored → the middle: "most people are
  just trying to build something and get treated fairly."

### T2 · THE PAYABLE-STAKES QUESTION (the mission pivot, one sentence)
- Mechanics: "We went looking for the real answers and found something
  much stranger than we expected. Whatever you think about AI, SOMEBODY
  IS ALREADY PAYING FOR IT. Who, and how much?" — the stakes are not
  vibes; they're attached to a RECEIPTABLE FACT. The question is
  answerable, and the video will answer it.
- Why it works: curiosity anchored to an unsettled BILL, not a threat.
- CF application: this is literally our metering pitch: "every token you
  prompt — somebody's GPU burned for it. Somebody's electricity. Somebody's
  bill. Who, and how much?"

### T3 · THE HISTORY INOCULATION (3:35 chapter, ~2 min)
- Mechanics: "the long history of being worried about computers and energy
  use" — the fear is OLD; context first, panic second. Calm = credibility.
- CF application: "every generation panics about the new machine" — the
  loom, the calculator, the demo-scene panic of the 90s. The open-source
  funding panic = this week's version.

### T4 · THE RULE BEFORE THE BREAK (4:57, the earned-drama move)
- Mechanics: teach the STABLE PATTERN first (Koomey's law: efficiency 2x
  every 1.6y for 80 years) — THEN break it ("today feels different"). The
  drama lands because the audience LEARNED the rule being broken.
- CF application: teach the open-source funding pattern (free code,
  forgotten payments, the maintainer economy) — THEN: "AI broke that
  equation too." Our Recreation Age essay's §II does this; in video form
  it becomes a beat, not a paragraph.

### T5 · THE STORY-CARRIED CONCEPT (9:12, the Jevons chapter)
- Mechanics: every abstract concept rides a NAMED HUMAN AND A STORY —
  Jevons himself, Victorian coal, "spoiler: this won't be the last time
  you hear from him" (the callback promise = a hook planted inside an
  explanation). Humor beats spread evenly = pacing release.
- CF application: our named humans already exist: the maintainer, the
  steward, the 703 prompt authors, Buckmaster's press-release war. Concepts
  ride people: "meet the man who built the package registry" (the Laurie
  Voss filing = real tape-class material).

### T6 · THE QUEST CHAIN (60% of the runtime: the mechanics chapters)
- Mechanics: each chapter answers the question the PREVIOUS chapter raised.
  "How much electricity?" → "where does it come from?" → "what if they
  build their own plant?" → "how do we even measure it?" No chapter without
  a question-parent. The audience chases; the video never lectures cold.
- CF application: fund → claim → meter → receipt → verified use → rent:
  each mechanism chapter opens with the question the last one minted.
  Never explain a mechanism before its question exists.

### T7 · THE EXPERT WITNESSES
- Mechanics: contested/technical claims get EXTERNAL VOICES on tape (the
  utility planner, the scientist) + a public sources page. The video never
  carries contested claims alone.
- CF application: the maintainer interviews (Mom-Test style), the P2
  creators on tape, the receipts page PUBLIC — our sources page = the
  receipts themselves (the receipts-marketing law: the ledger = the
  video's citations).

- QUOTES-AS-WITNESSES (upgraded 10/25, Gui's standing rule): real quotes from
  REAL PEOPLE are first-class scripting units - named on screen ("said by Sam
  Altman, January 2025"), delivered by their own voice or THE NARRATOR reading
  the quote, each traced to a source file. Attributed quotes = the script's
  receipts, spoken out loud.### T8 · THE HONEST LIMITS BEAT (the trust peak)
- Mechanics: say ON AIR what nobody can measure yet — "the scientific
  community is nowhere near being able to measure the net effect." The
  audience trusts MORE when the presenter refuses to overclaim. (The
  anti-guru move: gurus never admit the meter's broken.)
- CF application: we say it plainly — "the platform does not promise
  returns; receipts prove work, not futures." The no-immunity law, and
  the walls, stated as strengths.

### T9 · THE STEELMANNED RESOLUTION (no strawmen, no villains)
- Mechanics: BOTH extremes get dismissed BY NAME with their own arguments
  (the "catastrophe" crowd AND the "almonds/golf consume more" crowd),
  using each side's best case. The resolution = not a product pitch — a
  CITIZENSHIP invitation: "no fair decision without a shared truth; we
  choose wisely."
- CF application: our no-villains law in video form — the Tower and the
  clone-culture get their steelman moments; the close = "people decide
  where projects go" = the invitation to join the deciding.

### T10 · THE SOURCES ON RECORD (the receipts of the video itself)
- Mechanics: a PUBLIC references page linked in description (PBS standard),
  every claim traceable.
- CF application: THE LAW for all our videos: every launch/mission/explainer
  video ships with a receipts page — timestamped sources, our own docs,
  the archive links. The video's receipts = the marketing (the receipts-
  first brand, applied to ourselves first).

## THE RATIOS (the producer's mix, from the source)
Hook ~5% · history+rules ~15% · quest-chain mechanics ~60% · witnesses+limits
~10% · steelman+invitation ~10%. Emotional beats: rare, short, placed at the
transitions (humor every ~3 min; the stakes-question = the single big beat).

## HOW TO USE THE TEMPLATE
1. Pick the PAYABLE-STAKES QUESTION first (T2) — the piece is built around
   one answerable unsettled bill. If you can't name it, it's not this
   template.
2. Outline the QUEST CHAIN (T6): every section must be born from the
   previous section's open question. Kill any section that doesn't.
3. Assign the witnesses (T7) and the limits (T8) honestly — sourcing page
   from day one (T10).
4. Mirror (T1) and steelman (T9) last but FIRST-drafted — the framing is
   the piece's spine.
5. Existing pieces that ALREADY follow it: the TED speech ≈ T1/T2/T4/T9
   (the emotional mix); The Recreation Age essay = T2/T3/T4/T9 in text.
   The upcoming "how it works" explainer = the full template's first big
   user. And note: the PBS piece pre-audiences our pitch — "who pays for
   AI" is chapter 1; the metering answer is chapter 2. A series shape:
   they asked the question this week; our video answers it next.

### THE CLIP-HUNTING TOOL-CHAIN (Gui's second reason for sharing — filed 10/26)
- **C-SPAN = a hunting TOOL**: congressional hearings (and program
  archives) with SEARCHABLE TRANSCRIPTS — keyword search returns the exact
  timestamp IN the video; the clip = CUTCABLE (the Waco lesson: the
  built-in timecodes can be wrong — verify by watching; see his
  optimization tip below)
- **Gui's keyword-optimization rule (his tip, adopted)**: search the
  PROCEDURAL phrases ("play the tape", "play the recording", "start the
  audio"), not the topic nouns — the clerk's vocabulary finds the moment
  in seconds instead of the full watch-through
- **YouTube = the same, self-hosted**: the site doesn't expose keyword-in-
  video search, BUT OUR PIPELINE HAS IT ALREADY: youtube-transcript-api
  returns timestamped segments — a keyword scan yields exact timestamps ->
  ffmpeg cuts the snippet. THE MINI-TOOL TO BUILD (any idle block):
  clip-hunter.py = video-id + keyword -> timestamped hits -> the clip
  file + the citation metadata (source + timestamp) ready for the
  description (R-VS3)
- **FOOTAGE-AS-SOURCES LAW** (the extension of quotes-as-witnesses): our
  investigation/explainer videos can carry ACTUAL FOOTAGE of people
  speaking (the sourced clips) — always with the on-screen/printed
  citation. C-SPAN caveat to verify before building on it: their terms of
  use for redistribution/clipping (their archive = private copyright with
  an open-history mission; the DMCA-exception the Waco video cites = the

- **Status**: the tooling lives in the video-UI lane; the rules live in

## ADDENDUM - THE HOOK LAYER (R-KW: the Kallaway Triple Hook, transcribed 10/26)
Every video (either template) opens with the triple hook layered onto beat 1:
- **R-KW1 - CONTEXT IMMEDIATELY**: the viewer must know what's coming within
  the first sentence; audio + visuals + caption say it in sync (their data:
  even delaying context to the back half of sentence one drives the skip).
- **R-KW2 - THE LEAN**: open the curiosity loop with a proof/stat, a named
  pain, or a shock - the viewer leans in rather than opts out.
- **R-KW3 - THE CONTRARIAN SNAPBACK**: yank: pull the leaning viewer
  sideways (the fishing-hook metaphor). Then the rest of the beats run.
(v2 script's cold open = verify against R-KW1..3 before render; hooks = the
template's ignition system.)
