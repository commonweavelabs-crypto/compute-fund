COMPUTE FUND — GAMING & THE LAW (narrator script v2, documentary, human)
Chunks below = the exact VoxCPM2 render manifest (docs/vox-manifest-gaming-v2.json).

[0 (calm, measured)] In the year 2000, Sony lost a lawsuit. Not to a rival giant — to a
small company with nine engineers, who wanted Mac owners to play PlayStation games on
their computers. Sony had the lawyers, the billions, and the most popular console on
Earth. Everyone assumed it would be over quickly. It wasn't.

[1 (contemplative)] The little company, Connectix, admitted what it had done. It took
Sony's console software apart to understand how the machine behaved — then wrote its
own code from scratch, copying none of it into the final product. The Ninth Circuit
looked at that and said: that is the way it's supposed to work. Learning from a machine
is lawful. Copying it is not. Sony's loss became everybody else's permission slip.

[2 (low, controlled)] It wasn't the first time. In 1992, Sega went after Accolade for
the same reason — Accolade had studied Sega's console so it could make its own
compatible games. The court sided with Accolade too. Both cases drew the same bright
line, the line written into United States copyright law itself: the law protects
expression — the art, the music, the code as written, the characters, the names. The
law does not protect how a machine works.

[3 (resolved)] For our creators, this is the foundation everything stands on. On
Compute Fund, rebuilding the way a program works — a decompilation, a recompilation, a
port, a compatibility layer — is legitimate engineering, and it has been for over
thirty years. What is never okay: ripped textures, ripped models, ripped music, someone
else's characters, someone else's trademarks. Make your own. License them. A technically
perfect rebuild with stolen art is still a violation — and our community is better
than that.

[4 (calm, measured)] So how does Compute Fund help? Here is the honest answer. We are
not a courtroom, and we never want to be. Think of our mechanisms as keys — each one
unlocks a part of the process, and each one guides you down the safe side of the line
the courts already drew. Follow the rules, and the road stays smooth. We make it
easier. We don't promise outcomes — we promise a map.

[5 (warm)] The first key opens every project. When you open a project, the content
agreement comes first — one plain-language page and one checkbox: I own the rights to
what I'm opening. It teaches as much as it protects. And if something ever goes wrong,
there's a fair path: a verified takedown gets honored, an honest mistake gets a fix
window and an appeal, and the receipts stay on the ledger.

[6 (calm, measured)] The second key is the fork. On Compute Fund, our community members
never copy each other — we fork. A fork takes over a working base, publicly, with
credit: the original author's line stays visible in the ledger forever. And when you
fork, you pay rent. Rent is a share of what your fork brings in — a percentage of your
funding that flows back to the project you built on. The original creators keep earning
from their work. That's the fair share, written into the machine.

[7 (warm)] Which brings us to what we believe. Compute Fund is open source, funding
open source — the missing piece. Open source gave the world its infrastructure; too
many of the builders never got paid. Here, if you do good work and the community likes
it, the community rewards you. You grow. That's our promise — contribution earns. Not a
promise about the law. A promise about us.

[8 (contemplative)] And there are friends to be made here. Merge your project with
another creator's project. Share what you build — everything on Compute Fund is
shareable, forkable, and monetizable. That word matters. Open source always had
sharing. It never quite had monetizing. This platform is the piece that was missing:
funding the open source, taking it seriously, paying its people.

[9 (resolved)] The permission the courts granted in 1992 and in 2000 still belongs to
our creators. The tools of 2026 make the building faster than it has ever been. And the
community makes it worth doing. Open a project. Fork what's open. Rebuild what you
love, clean-room, with your own art. Get funded, on the record. The members of Compute
Fund are building the next generation of software in the open — and there is room in it
for you. What a time to be alive.
